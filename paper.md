# Hubs for Free: What Commutator-Based Analyses of Attention Discover in Random Matrices

**Author:** Stefan Ojanen (draft v0.1, August 2026)
**Code and data:** `experiments.py`, `run_qwen_protocol.py`, `analyze_local.py`, `results.json`, `local_results.json`, `qwen_results.json` (all synthetic results in this paper regenerate from a single script in under one minute on CPU; the Qwen protocol runs in minutes on a laptop)

## Abstract

A growing genre of work analyzes transformer attention with tools imported from physics: commutator "field strengths" between heads, Lie-algebra extraction, spectral L-functions, and symbolic regression on the resulting coefficients. Our own earlier analysis of Qwen2.5-0.5B in this style reported an (n-1)+1 organization of attention heads, a single "coordinator" dimension coupling to all others, a layer-wise "crystallization" of commutator norms, and a compact symbolic law governing Lie-algebra structure constants. Here we show that every one of these signatures is produced, at comparable or higher strength, by random causal softmax matrices containing no learned structure. We give four short analytical results explaining why: (1) row-stochasticity forces one shared invariant direction on any set of attention matrices, so a "1 universal + (n-1) specialized" split is a constraint of softmax, not a discovery; (2) for causal attention the skew-generator norm obeys an exact identity, 2||G_h||_F^2 = sum_i [IPR_i - A_ii^2], so "generator energy" measures nothing more than sharp non-self attention; (3) the pairwise commutator-norm matrix is bounded by and empirically concentrates on the rank-1 matrix of norm products (correlation 0.93 to 0.97 on noise), which guarantees a single dominant eigenvalue and a "hub" element by Perron-Frobenius; (4) the standard 2-sigma outlier rule flags a "special dimension" in 48 to 79 percent of pure-noise trials at n = 14, rising to 97 percent at n = 32. A formula search on structure constants extracted from noise reaches best R^2 between 0.010 and 0.032 across runs; the logged R^2 of the real Qwen analysis, 0.014, sits inside this null distribution, and the structure constants themselves are not well-defined regression targets because an arbitrary SVD sign convention scrambles them (correlation 0.10 between two equally valid conventions). Finally, zeros of Dirichlet polynomials built from random positive "layer energies" cluster around Re(s) = 0.48 and exhibit Wigner-like spacing statistics, reproducing the claimed "Neural Riemann Hypothesis" signatures with no model in the loop. We then execute a preregistered pilot of the protocol on a transformer trained from scratch: the hub, eigengap, and rank-1 signatures on the trained model are matched at the 90 to 96 percent level by marginal-preserving surrogates, the identity of the "coordinator" head is stable on only 37 percent of inputs, and no symbolic law is found (real best R^2 at or below the matched-noise search); yet the battery also detects two small genuine signals, a systematic deviation of the trained coupling matrix from its surrogates and an ablation effect concentrated on the modal max-norm head (z = 3.9), demonstrating that the protocol has power to find real structure when it exists. We conclude with a null-model checklist for structure claims about attention.

## 1. Introduction

Interpretability research increasingly borrows formal machinery from physics and pure mathematics. Attention matrices get treated as gauge connections, their commutators as field strengths, their skew-symmetric parts as Lie-algebra generators, their layer statistics as coefficients of L-functions. The appeal is obvious: if the analogy holds, decades of theory become available. The risk is equally obvious but less discussed: these statistics have strong generic behavior on arbitrary matrices, and a pipeline that never runs on a null model cannot distinguish learned structure from the geometry of the ambient space.

This paper is a self-correction. In earlier work we applied exactly such a pipeline to Qwen2.5-0.5B and reported four findings: a "(n-1)+1" organization in which one of 14 attention-head dimensions couples to all others; a dramatic eigengap separating that dimension from the rest; a monotone decrease of mean commutator norm across depth, interpreted as thermodynamic "cooling" into an ordered phase; and a compact symbolic law for the Lie-algebra structure constants of layer 21. Re-examination of our own logs, followed by the experiments below, shows that each observation is either a mathematical necessity of softmax attention, a base-rate event under standard outlier rules, or a fit whose quality matches what identical searches achieve on noise. The reported headline law (R^2 = 0.982) does not appear in the archived symbolic-regression logs at all; the archived best fit improves on the constant predictor y = 0 by 1.4 percent, and the published formula violates a hard bound (|f| <= 2 for orthonormal generators) by four orders of magnitude, so it cannot describe the data it was attributed to.

Rather than only retracting, we convert the episode into something reusable: a set of short proofs and a battery of null experiments that any commutator-, spectrum-, or symbolic-regression-based claim about attention should clear before interpretation. All experiments run on synthetic matrices in seconds; a companion script gives a preregistered protocol for the real-model questions that remain open.

**Contributions.**
1. An exact identity tying skew-generator norm to attention sharpness and self-attention mass (Section 3.2), which reduces "generator energy" analyses to two mundane per-head statistics.
2. A proof that softmax attention always carries one shared invariant direction, making any "1 universal + (n-1)" decomposition non-evidential (Section 3.1).
3. A bound plus empirical concentration result showing pairwise commutator-norm matrices are approximately rank-1 in the generator norms, which guarantees hub-and-spectral-gap findings on arbitrary data (Section 3.3), with base rates for outlier rules across n (Section 4.2).
4. A demonstration that formula search on structure constants extracted from noise matches the published real-model fit quality, and that the regression target itself is convention-dependent (Section 4.3).
5. A null for spectral "L-function" claims: random coefficients reproduce critical-line clustering and Wigner-like spacing (Section 4.5).
6. A checklist and preregistered real-model protocol with an executed pilot (Sections 6, 7).

We emphasize what we do not claim. Trained attention certainly contains structure; sinks, positional heads, induction heads, and specialized circuits are well documented. Our claim is narrower and methodological: the statistics above cannot detect that structure, because they return the same answers on matrices that have none.

## 2. Setup and notation

An attention head on a length-T sequence produces a row-stochastic matrix A in R^{T x T}, A_ij >= 0, sum_j A_ij = 1, with A lower-triangular in the causal case. For a layer with heads {A_h}, h = 1..n, prior work defines: the skew generators G_h = (A_h - A_h^T)/2; the coupling (or "field strength") matrix C_ij = ||[G_i, G_j]||_F (some analyses use raw A_h; our results cover both); an orthonormal basis {B_k} for span{G_h} obtained by SVD of the stacked, vectorized generators; and structure constants f_ijk = <B_k, [B_i, B_j]>_F. The published pipeline then applies a 2-sigma outlier rule to generator norms and coupling row-sums, eigendecomposes C, Ward-clusters 1 - C/max(C), and runs symbolic regression of f_ijk on the index triple (i, j, k).

Our null ensembles contain no training and no semantics: (i) Gaussian-logit causal softmax with per-head temperatures drawn lognormally, (ii) the same with a first-token logit bias on 30 percent of heads (a minimal stand-in for the ubiquitous sink pattern, though no result depends on it), (iii) Dirichlet-distributed rows. Default n = 14 heads, T = 26 tokens, matching the analyzed Qwen configuration. All numbers below are in `results.json`; figures regenerate from `experiments.py`.

## 3. Analytical results

### 3.1 One shared direction is a constraint, not a discovery

**Proposition 1.** Let A, B be row-stochastic. Then (AB)1 = 1 and [A, B]1 = 0, where 1 is the all-ones vector.

*Proof.* A1 = 1 and B1 = 1 by row-stochasticity, so (AB)1 = A(B1) = 1 and [A,B]1 = AB1 - BA1 = 1 - 1 = 0.

Every product of attention matrices fixes the uniform direction and every commutator annihilates it (verified numerically to 5e-16). Any analysis of head interactions built from products or commutators therefore lives in the (T-1)-dimensional complement of a single direction shared by all heads. A decomposition into "one universal dimension plus the rest" is present in every softmax attention layer ever instantiated, trained or not.

### 3.2 Generator norm is sharp non-self attention (exact identity)

**Proposition 2.** For causal (lower-triangular) row-stochastic A with skew part G = (A - A^T)/2,

2 ||G||_F^2 = sum_i [ IPR_i - A_ii^2 ],  where IPR_i = sum_j A_ij^2.

*Proof.* ||G||_F^2 = (1/4)||A - A^T||_F^2 = (1/2)(||A||_F^2 - Tr(A^2)). For lower-triangular A, A_ij A_ji = 0 unless i = j, so Tr(A^2) = sum_i A_ii^2, and ||A||_F^2 = sum_i IPR_i.

IPR_i is the collision probability of row i, a standard sharpness measure. The identity holds to 7e-15 across 700 random heads (Figure 2). Its content: the quantity treated as "Lie-algebraic energy" of a head is exactly (attention sharpness) minus (self-attention mass), summed over queries. A head that attends sharply to any token other than itself (a sink head attending to position 0 is the canonical example) maximizes it; a head that attends to itself or diffusely minimizes it. No algebra is being measured.

### 3.3 The coupling matrix is approximately rank-1, so a hub is guaranteed

**Proposition 3.** C_ij = ||[G_i, G_j]||_F <= 2 ||G_i||_F ||G_j||_F.

*Proof.* ||XY||_F <= ||X||_2 ||Y||_F <= ||X||_F ||Y||_F and the triangle inequality.

Empirically the bound is nearly saturated up to a common factor: across our ensembles the correlation between off-diagonal C_ij and the rank-1 matrix ||G_i|| ||G_j|| has median 0.93 to 0.97 (Section 4.1). A symmetric nonnegative matrix that is approximately rank-1 has, by Perron-Frobenius and eigenvalue perturbation, a single dominant eigenvalue with eigenvector approximately proportional to the norm vector, a large gap to the bulk, and row-sums approximately proportional to the norms. Consequences, all guaranteed rather than emergent: the largest-norm element "couples to everyone"; the eigenspectrum shows "one versus (n-1)"; and by Proposition 2 the hub is simply the head with the sharpest non-self attention.

### 3.4 Outlier rules have high base rates at these n

The published pipeline flags a "special dimension" when any of n values (generator norms or coupling row-sums) deviates from the sample mean by more than two sample standard deviations. For i.i.d. draws this fires on some index with substantial probability at n = 14 and near-certainty by n = 32; heterogeneous per-head temperatures (present in any real model) inflate the rate further. Measured rates are in Section 4.2. A rule that fires on half of noise draws cannot serve as evidence of organization.

## 4. Null experiments

### 4.1 Hub-for-free (Figure 1)

300 trials per ensemble at n = 14, T = 26, full pipeline. Median corr(C_ij, ||G_i|| ||G_j||): 0.97 (Gaussian), 0.93 (Gaussian + sink), 0.93 (Dirichlet). Median top-eigenvalue gap lambda_1/|lambda_2|: 51, 66, 31. Probability that the coupling hub (argmax row-sum) is the max-norm head: 0.83, 0.62, 0.84. Ward clustering into 4 groups yields the signature "three singletons plus one large cluster" pattern in 86, 96, and 46 percent of trials respectively; the published real-model clustering [1,1,1,11] is thus the modal outcome of this procedure on noise.

### 4.2 Base rates and universality across n (Figure 3)

Probability that the 2-sigma rule flags at least one "special dimension" on pure noise: 0.37 (n = 8), 0.59 (12), 0.75 (14), 0.75 (16), 0.77 (17), 0.96 (24), 0.97 (32). The probability that the flagged set is exactly one shared index is 0.44 to 0.71 at n = 14 depending on ensemble. Median eigengap grows roughly linearly in n (19 at n = 8 to 184 at n = 32), as the rank-1 picture predicts. The normalized spectra display the "one dominant plus bulk" shape at every n tested: the "(n-1)+1" pattern is not a property of n = 14 or n = 17; it is a property of the statistic.

As a concrete exhibit of base rates rather than a claim of coincidence: random seed 147 of the Gaussian ensemble reproduces the previously published real-model summary in every particular, with a unique 2-sigma outlier at index 9, eigengap 68, and Ward clustering [1,1,1,11]. The probability that a noise draw places its unique outlier at any prespecified index is 1/14; nothing about index 9 is special.

### 4.3 Structure constants and symbolic laws (Figure 4)

Running the published SVD-basis extraction on a noise draw yields 2,178 above-threshold structure constants with RMS 0.025 and max |f| = 0.124, consistent with the hard bound |f_ijk| <= 2 that follows from Proposition 3 with orthonormal generators. Two results:

**The target is convention-dependent.** The SVD basis is defined only up to per-vector sign. Under a random sign assignment epsilon in {+-1}^n, the constants transform as f_ijk -> epsilon_i epsilon_j epsilon_k f_ijk; the correlation between the constants under two equally valid conventions is 0.10 in our draw. Separately, the SVD basis is invariant to relabeling the input heads (it depends only on sum_h vec(G_h) vec(G_h)^T), so the indices i, j, k do not refer to heads at all: they index principal components of one input's generator set, ordered by singular value. A "law" in these indices is a statement about an ordering convention applied to a single sample.

**Fit quality on noise matches the archived real fit.** A formula search over the same operator set used previously ({+, -, *, /, sin, cos, exp}), with affine leaves and constant hill-climbing, achieves best R^2 between 0.010 and 0.032 (median 0.022) across three noise datasets and two seeds each. The archived PySR logs of the real Qwen layer-21 run show a best loss of 1.696e-3 against 1.720e-3 for the constant predictor y = 0, i.e. R^2 = 0.014, inside the noise distribution. The previously published equation, f = [j(i - 0.32) - (-k + exp(sin i))]/0.0079 with R^2 = 0.982, appears nowhere in those logs, and over the index grid it takes values in [-651, 22263], outside the feasible range |f| <= 2 by four orders of magnitude; whatever produced that expression, it was not this data.

### 4.4 Depth-wise "crystallization" from a locality schedule (Figure 5)

Using the raw-attention commutator statistic of the original pipeline, we sweep 24 pseudo-layers of random matrices whose only systematic property is a schedule moving attention mass from the first column (sink-like, early) to the diagonal (self-attention, late). Mean pairwise F falls 19-fold from 0.56 to 0.03, tracking mean diagonal mass, with a mild non-monotone hump mid-schedule; no training is involved. Combined with Proposition 2, a depth-decreasing commutator norm is a restatement of the well-documented tendency of attention to become more local and self-focused with depth, and supports no thermodynamic reading. (Separately, the previously reported depth means, 45.2 falling to 4.2, could not be reproduced from the archived per-head energy table, whose values imply per-pair F of order 1 to 3 at layer 0; the re-measurement under the released script is in Section 7, P5.)

### 4.5 The "Neural L-function" (Figure 6)

Define L(s) = sum_{m=1}^{24} a_m m^{-s} with a_m random positive "layer energies" (|N(0,1)| + 0.1), five draws. Newton-refined zero-finding over Re(s) in [-0.5, 1.6], Im(s) in (0, 140] locates 273 zeros. Their real parts center at 0.48; the unfolded nearest-neighbor spacings of the imaginary parts show clear level repulsion and track the Wigner (GOE) surmise far better than Poisson. Both previously cited signatures of a "Neural Riemann Hypothesis" (zeros near the critical line; Wigner-Dyson spacing) therefore arise from random coefficients with no model in the loop. This is unsurprising: level repulsion is generic for zeros of random analytic functions (Bogomolny et al., 1992; Hannay, 1996), and the horizontal distribution of zeros of truncated Dirichlet series is a classical subject in which zeros concentrate in narrow vertical strips determined by the coefficients (Spira, 1966; Borwein et al., 2007).

### 4.6 Ratio numerology base rates

The published golden-ratio observation was |9/14 - 1/phi| = 0.0248. Using exactly that tolerance and a modest library of twelve "meaningful" constants (1/2, 1/3, 2/3, 1/e, 1 - 1/e, 1/phi, 1/sqrt2, ln 2, pi/4, 1/pi, 3/5, 5/8), 36 percent of all possible outlier positions at n = 14 match some constant, and likewise 35 percent at n = 17. The associated "prediction" that the special dimension of an SO(17) analysis lies at floor(17/phi) +- 1 covers 3 of 17 positions, an 18 percent hit rate under the null. Post-hoc ratio matching at these tolerances is uninformative.

## 5. Case study: the published summary versus the null

The previously circulated 14-dimensional analysis summary made five empirical claims about Qwen2.5-0.5B layer 21: a clear 13+1 structure; dimension 9 as unique outlier in both norms and coupling; a dramatic eigengap; a coupling matrix in which dimension 9 "talks to all others"; and Ward clustering [1,1,1,11]. Every element of that summary is reproduced verbatim by noise draw seed 147 of our Gaussian ensemble (Section 4.2), and each element individually occurs on noise with probability between 0.44 and 0.96 under the same decision rules. The single quantitative real-model number that survives against its own archived logs, the symbolic-fit R^2 of 0.014, sits inside the noise distribution of the identical search (Section 4.3). On present evidence, no statistic in the pipeline distinguishes the trained model from random matrices. This does not show layer 21 is unstructured; it shows these instruments cannot see structure.

## 6. A null-battery checklist for attention "structure" claims

1. **Constraint check.** Derive what the statistic does on the constraint set alone (row-stochasticity, causality, nonnegativity) before interpreting it. Propositions 1 and 2 took four lines each.
2. **Matched-null battery.** Run the identical pipeline on (a) random matrices from the constraint set, (b) per-head marginal-matched surrogates, (c) an untrained model of the same architecture. Report the real statistic as a percentile of the null distribution, never as a bare number.
3. **Convention invariance.** Any claimed law must survive basis sign flips, head relabeling, and index permutation, or it describes the convention.
4. **Base-rate audit of decision rules.** Publish P(flag | noise) for every outlier or clustering rule at the actual n.
5. **Effect-size floor for searched fits.** For symbolic regression or any adaptive fit, report the best fit the same search achieves on matched noise; a real fit is evidence only above that line.
6. **Multi-input distributions.** One forward pass on one sentence characterizes (model, sentence). Report distributions over at least 10^3 diverse inputs, and stability of any identified "special" component across them.
7. **Ledger discipline.** Every number in the text must be regenerable from an archived script and log; searched exhibits (like our seed 147) must be labeled as searched.

## 7. Preregistered protocol and a local pilot

The deflationary results above leave real questions open. We preregistered six predictions (P1 to P6 below), then executed the full battery on a transformer trained from scratch in the same environment: a byte-level GPT (4 layers, 8 heads, d = 128, context 64, 0.85M parameters) trained for 2,000 steps on 6.8 MB of source code and documentation, reaching validation loss 2.46 from 4.85 at initialization. The battery compares the trained model, its untrained initialization, and per-head marginal-matched surrogates (each row's off-diagonal causal entries permuted, which preserves every row's IPR and diagonal mass exactly and therefore all Proposition-2 statistics). The companion script `run_qwen_protocol.py` applies the identical protocol to Qwen2.5-0.5B; those numbers have now been collected (150 WikiText-103 validation texts truncated to 64 tokens, focal layer 21, float32 weights, eager attention; all values below are in `qwen_results.json` and regenerate from the script). Predictions are quoted as registered, followed by the pilot outcome and the Qwen outcome.

**P1 (identity of the outlier).** *Registered:* the max-generator-norm head is the sink head; corr(generator norm, first-token mass) > 0.8. *Outcome: the identity holds, the sink guess fails.* The exact identity 2||G||^2 = sum_i[IPR_i - A_ii^2] verifies to 7.6e-6 (float32). But on the trained model corr(gnorm, sink mass) = -0.10 across heads (0.71 on the untrained model), and the modal max-norm head (head 6) is not the sink head (head 3). Proposition 2's actual content, sharp non-self attention, stands; our over-specific guess that this would be first-token attention does not, at least in this model. Coarse offset profiles (self, previous-token, first-token mass) do not distinguish the hub head at this layer. *Qwen outcome: held.* The identity verifies to 1.5e-4 (float32 at T = 64). The mean across-head correlation between generator norm and first-token mass over 150 inputs is 0.98, above the registered 0.8, and the modal max-norm head is the max-sink head (head 11 of 14). At Qwen layer 21 the registered guess is exactly right: the head this pipeline crowns as coordinator is the attention sink.

**P2 (stability).** *Registered:* an input-unstable outlier is noise; a stable one is a known head type. *Outcome: weak stability.* The argmax-norm head at the focal layer is head 6 on 37 percent of 200 held-out inputs, with head 1 at 30 percent and two others above 10 percent (untrained baseline: 50 percent for its modal head, since fixed random weights are also input-consistent). The "coordinator" identity flips across inputs even in a trained model; any single-coordinator narrative would have to survive this instability. *Qwen outcome: held, on the stable branch.* The argmax-norm head at layer 21 is head 11 on 150 of 150 inputs (pilot: 37 percent). Perfect stability, and a known head type: by P1 this head is the layer's attention sink. The registered dichotomy resolves cleanly, a stable outlier that is a documented phenomenon rather than a new form of organization.

**P3 (surrogate parity).** *Registered:* marginal-matched surrogates reproduce the coupling statistics within null variability; failure would be genuine evidence of structure. *Outcome: parity approximately holds in magnitude, and fails in a small, systematic way.* Median eigengap 13.8 (real) versus 11.5 (surrogates); median rank-1 correlation 0.90 (real) versus 0.96 (surrogates). So roughly 90 to 96 percent of the hub-and-gap phenomenology on a trained model is reproduced by per-head marginals alone. However, the real gap falls inside the surrogate min-max range on only 10 percent of windows (78 percent expected under exchangeability with 8 surrogates), so a residual pairwise signal beyond marginals exists: real head pairs are less rank-1 than their surrogates, indicating specific inter-head alignment. This is the first statistic in the entire pipeline to separate trained attention from its nulls, and it is small.
*Qwen outcome: failed, in the direction of genuine structure, and far more strongly than in the pilot.* Median eigengap over 10 windows: 38.5 (real) versus 31.3 (80 surrogates), with the real gap inside the surrogate min-max range on 20 percent of windows (78 percent expected under exchangeability). The sharper result is the rank-1 statistic: median corr(C_ij, ||G_i|| ||G_j||) is -0.04 on the real model versus 0.985 on its surrogates. The pilot's 0.90-versus-0.96 deficit becomes, at Qwen scale, a complete failure of norm-product concentration: the trained coupling matrix's off-diagonal pattern carries essentially no imprint of the generator norms, while marginal-matched surrogates of the same windows are almost perfectly rank-1. Real head pairs at this layer are far less rank-1 than any per-head marginal explanation allows, which indicates strong, specific inter-head alignment. Given P1, the natural candidate is shared sink structure, since generators that share a dominant first-column direction can have anomalously small mutual commutators, but establishing the mechanism needs its own null and is future work. Two deflationary conclusions survive intact: the eigengap by itself remains uninformative, since surrogates produce a comparable gap from marginals alone, and the hub identity remains the sink head. What separates the trained model from its nulls is a statistic the original pipeline never computed.

**P4 (symbolic search parity).** *Registered:* formula search on real and noise structure constants yields overlapping quality. *Outcome: holds.* Best searched R^2 on the trained model's focal-layer constants: 0.026 and 0.047 (two seeds); on shape-matched noise: 0.086 and 0.097. No symbolic law; the noise search actually fits more, as expected with fewer constants (roughly 320 points here). *Qwen outcome: held.* The layer-21 extraction yields 2,184 above-threshold constants. Best searched R^2: 0.0095 and 0.0125 on the real constants (two seeds), against 0.0104 and 0.0225 on shape-matched noise. The archived PySR fit of the original analysis, R^2 = 0.014, sits inside both ranges. At the exact source of the original claim, the search on the real model does not outperform the identical search on noise, and no symbolic law exists.

**P5 (depth curve from locality).** *Registered:* mean F across layers is predicted by mean diagonal mass and mean IPR with R^2 > 0.9. *Outcome: partially holds.* Across 800 (layer, input) pairs, locality explains R^2 = 0.65 of mean-F variance in the trained model (0.04 in the untrained model, which has essentially no depth variation to explain: mean F 0.025 to 0.028 flat). Two readings, both honest: the single largest driver of "field strength" is attention sharpening, since training multiplies mean F roughly 50-fold (0.03 to 1.3-1.8) exactly as Proposition 2 predicts; and a third of the variance is not locality, so the registered threshold fails and F carries some information beyond the two mundane statistics in this model. With four layers this is a weak instrument; the 24-layer Qwen run is the decisive version.
*Qwen outcome: partial, as in the pilot.* Across 2,400 (layer, input) pairs (24 layers, 100 inputs), diagonal mass and mean IPR explain R^2 = 0.70 of mean-F variance, short of the registered 0.9 (pilot: 0.65). The re-measured depth profile is incompatible with the original crystallization narrative: mean F under the protocol's generator-commutator statistic is not monotone in depth, running near 1.0 over the first three layers, oscillating between 1.8 and 4.5 through the middle of the network, and dropping to 0.86 and 0.66 only in the final two layers. The originally reported monotone 45.2-to-4.2 decline was computed on raw-attention commutators, so the two statistics are not directly comparable, but under the released protocol there is no monotone decline to explain, and per-layer means span 0.66 to 4.5, with nothing near the archived starting value of 45.2. Roughly 30 percent of mean-F variance is not captured by the two locality statistics, echoing the pilot's residual; that residual, not any depth trend, is the part worth pursuing.

**P6 (causal role).** *Registered:* ablating the max-norm head lands within the distribution of random single-head ablations, or any excess traces to sink behavior. *Outcome: fails in the interesting direction.* Ablating modal hub head 6 at the focal layer raises validation loss by 0.030 nats, the largest of all 8 heads (z = 3.9 against the other seven; base loss 2.32, so a 1.3 percent effect). The excess is not sink-attributable (P1). The max-norm head is functionally the most load-bearing single head at this layer, while its identity remains input-dependent (P2). *Qwen outcome: held, on the null branch, and opposite to the pilot.* Ablating the modal max-norm head (head 11, the sink head) at layer 21 changes validation loss by -0.0009 nats against a base of 3.059, z = -1.28 relative to five random single-head ablations at the same layer (deltas -0.0005 to +0.0056). The head this pipeline identifies as the coordinator is causally inert under single-head ablation, consistent with sink heads functioning as no-ops (Xiao et al., 2023; Bondarenko et al., 2023). The pilot's load-bearing hub (z = 3.9) does not replicate at Qwen layer 21; the two models place their max-norm heads at opposite ends of the ablation distribution, which is itself evidence that maximal generator norm does not track functional importance.

**Pilot summary.** On a genuinely trained transformer, the pipeline's headline signatures (hub, eigengap, rank-1 coupling, "(n-1)+1" spectrum) are 90 to 96 percent reproduced by surrogates that know only each head's per-row sharpness and self-mass, the coordinator's identity is unstable across inputs, and no symbolic law exists. At the same time, the battery detected two small real effects, the P3 surrogate deviation and the P6 ablation excess, which is what distinguishes this protocol from pure debunking: it has power. The constructive research program is exactly those residuals: characterize the pairwise alignment that breaks rank-1, and the function of high-gnorm heads, on full-scale models. Failure of P3, P4, or P5 at Qwen scale, in either direction, is the next measurement that matters; the script is provided.

**Qwen2.5-0.5B summary.** The registered Qwen run closes the loop on the original analysis. The "coordinator" of the earlier work resolves into the attention sink of layer 21: it is the max-generator-norm head because sink attention is the sharpest non-self attention (P1, corr 0.98), it is perfectly stable across inputs precisely because it is a fixed structural head rather than an input-dependent one (P2, 150 of 150), and it is causally inert under single-head ablation (P6, z = -1.28). No symbolic law exists at the source of the original claim (P4, real search at or below the matched-noise search, archived fit inside both), and the depth profile is non-monotone, with locality explaining 70 percent of its variance (P5). The one genuine signal is P3, and it is larger than the pilot suggested: the trained coupling matrix abandons the norm-product geometry entirely (rank-1 correlation -0.04 real versus 0.985 surrogate), so real inter-head alignment at this layer is strong, specific, and invisible to every statistic in the original pipeline. Scorecard against the registered predictions: P1 held, P2 held on the stable branch, P3 failed toward structure, P4 held, P5 partial, P6 held on the null branch. The constructive program stands as stated above: characterize the alignment that breaks rank-1. For the ledger: run of August 2026 on CPU, transformers 5.15.0, torch 2.13.0, weights loaded in float32 (transformers 5 otherwise defaults to the checkpoint's bfloat16, which older versions upcast), dataset id in its namespaced form Salesforce/wikitext; both settings are in the archived script, and every number in this section regenerates from it into `qwen_results.json`.

## 8. Related work

Single dominant components in transformers are well documented under other names: attention sinks and the outsized role of initial tokens (Xiao et al., 2023), massive activations acting as universal bias-like hubs (Sun et al., 2024), register tokens in vision transformers (Darcet et al., 2023), vertical/no-op attention patterns (Clark et al., 2019; Kovaleva et al., 2019; Bondarenko et al., 2023), and rogue dimensions dominating similarity measures (Timkey and van Schijndel, 2021). Head redundancy and prunability are established (Michel et al., 2019; Voita et al., 2019). Our contribution is not to re-document these phenomena but to show that a popular class of physics-style statistics rediscovers them, or nothing, while appearing to discover algebraic organization. On the epistemics of spurious findings, Schaeffer et al. (2023) showed metric choices can manufacture emergent abilities; we show pipeline choices can manufacture emergent geometry. On symbolic regression, the overfitting behavior of expression search on small noisy datasets motivates our matched-noise floor (Cranmer, 2023). Genuine phase-transition theory for attention exists in solvable settings (Cui et al., 2024) and is untouched by our critique, which targets measurement, not theory.

## 9. Limitations

Our synthetic ensembles are simple, and the executed pilot uses a 0.85M-parameter byte-level model with 4 layers and 8 heads trained for 2,000 steps; effect sizes and stabilities do differ at scale, as the executed Qwen2.5-0.5B run shows (P6 reversed, P3 amplified; Section 7). The pilot's P3 and P6 signals, while systematic, are small (a 0.06 rank-1 correlation deficit; a 1.3 percent loss effect), and both the pilot and the Qwen run concern one focal layer each; the Qwen P3 and P6 results rest on 10 and 40 evaluation windows respectively at T = 64, and longer contexts, more layers, and more windows are the obvious extensions. We analyze the specific pipeline of the case study; other physics-inspired analyses may make invariant, well-nulled claims, and nothing here argues against importing mathematical structure per se, only against skipping the null. Finally, absence of evidence under these statistics is not evidence of absence: layers plainly contain circuits these instruments cannot resolve.

## 10. Conclusion

Four short derivations and twenty seconds of CPU suffice to generate, from random matrices, every headline signature of an analysis we previously believed revealed emergent algebraic organization in a transformer: the (n-1)+1 split, the universal coordinator, the spectral gap, the crystallizing depth profile, the symbolic law, the critical line, the golden ratio. The correct conclusion is not that transformers are unstructured but that these instruments measure the container, not the contents. We offer the checklist of Section 6 as the minimum bar for structure claims about attention, and the protocol of Section 7 as the honest continuation of the original research question: not "do transformers hide a gauge theory," but "which per-head statistics survive their own nulls, and what do the survivors do."
